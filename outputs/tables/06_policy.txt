==============================================================================
STAGE 6: RETENTION TARGETING POLICY
==============================================================================

  Every pound figure below rests on three numbers the data cannot
  supply. They are assumptions, set in config/policy.yaml:

    customer value : GBP 300 gross margin per retained customer per year
    save rate      : 25% of would-be churners retained by the offer
    offer cost     : GBP 40 per customer CONTACTED (not per save)
    horizon        : 1 year (deliberately not lifetime value)

  Population : the 2,026-customer held-out test set (16.0% churn).
  Everything is out-of-sample. Scaled projections to the full
  10,127-customer book are labelled as such.

==============================================================================
1. THE DECISION RULE
==============================================================================

    expected value = P(churn) x save_rate x customer_value - offer_cost

  Contact a customer when that is positive. Expected value rises with
  P(churn), so this is the same as a probability threshold, and the
  threshold has a closed form:

    break-even P(churn) = offer_cost / (save_rate x value)
                        = 40 / (0.25 x 300)
                        = 0.5333

  So a customer is worth contacting only if they are more than
  53.3% likely to churn.

  The recommended model's scores on this population run 0.052 to 0.561,
  with 1 customer at or above 53.3%.

==============================================================================
2. THE CENTRAL RESULT
==============================================================================

  policy                        contacted  exp. saves     net value  per contact
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  target nobody                         0         0.0         GBP 0        GBP 0
  target everyone                   2,026        81.8   -GBP 56,489      -GBP 28
  optimal (p >= 0.533)                  1         0.1         GBP 2        GBP 2
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Bootstrap 95% CI on the optimal policy's net value:
    GBP 2  [GBP 0, GBP 6]
    positive in 63% of resamples

  (That interval reflects only who happens to be in the population. It
  does NOT include uncertainty in the three assumptions, which is far
  larger -- see section 4.)

  VERDICT UNDER CENTRAL ASSUMPTIONS: technically positive, but it
  targets only 1 customer for GBP 2. That is not a campaign; it is a
  rounding error, and it would not survive the cost of building the
  pipeline to deliver it.

  Scaled to the full 10,127-customer book: GBP 10 per year.
  (Straight-line scaling, which assumes the book looks like the test
  set. It does, by construction of the split -- but a real book would
  need re-scoring rather than multiplying.)

==============================================================================
3. WHAT THE LEAKY MODEL WOULD HAVE PROMISED
==============================================================================

  The all-features model scored PR-AUC 0.97 and is almost certainly
  reading the answer. Here is what that would have looked like in a
  business case, beside what the defensible model actually delivers.

  model                               contacted     net value   scaled to book
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  all-features (leaky, PR-AUC 0.97)         322     GBP 9,497       GBP 47,472
  strict (defensible, PR-AUC 0.28)            1         GBP 2           GBP 10
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The leaky model promises GBP 47,461 a year more than the
  defensible one. That gap is the size of the business case that would
  have been signed off on a number which does not survive production --
  and it is the clearest answer to 'why bother with a leakage audit'.

==============================================================================
5. HOW WRONG CAN THE ASSUMPTIONS BE?
==============================================================================

  Each parameter moved on its own, with the other two held at central,
  to find where the campaign stops paying. This is the number to give a
  sceptical stakeholder: not 'trust me', but 'here is the boundary'.

  customer value must exceed GBP 285   (central GBP 300, ABOVE the line)
  save rate must exceed      23.8%        (central 25%, ABOVE the line)
  offer cost must stay below GBP 42   (central GBP 40, BELOW the line)

  SLACK ON EACH ASSUMPTION (how far central sits from the boundary):

    customer_value       +4.9%
    save_rate            +4.9%
    offer_cost           +5.1%

  Every one of those margins is under 15%, and the tightest is
  4.9%. Read together they are the real finding of this
  stage: the campaign is not profitable-with-a-margin, it is
  sitting on the break-even line in all three directions at once.

  A business case that survives only while three independent
  guesses all stay within a few percent of where I happened to
  set them is not a business case. It is an argument for
  measuring the save rate before spending anything.

==============================================================================
6. RECOMMENDATION
==============================================================================

  DO NOT run a blanket retention campaign on this model at these
  assumptions. Say so plainly rather than tuning the assumptions
  until the answer changes.

  What would make it viable, in order of how easily the business
  can move each lever:

  1. CUT THE OFFER COST below GBP 42. The cheapest lever
     and the one marketing controls directly. A points nudge
     rather than a fee waiver changes the arithmetic immediately.
  2. TARGET HIGHER-VALUE CUSTOMERS. At GBP 285+ of annual
     margin the campaign pays. Segment the book by value first
     and run retention only on the top tier, where the same
     offer cost buys much more.
  3. GET A BETTER MODEL, which means better DATA -- specifically
     behavioural features with a window that provably closes before
     the prediction date. Stage 3 showed the ceiling here is the
     data, not the algorithm.
  4. MEASURE THE SAVE RATE instead of assuming it. It is the most
     uncertain input and the stage 7 experiment is how to pin it.

  And the limitation that outranks all of the above:

  P(churn) IS NOT P(RESPONDS TO OFFER). This entire policy assumes the
  customers most likely to leave are the ones an offer can retain. They
  may be the least persuadable -- already decided, and now collecting a
  discount on their way out. Nothing in this dataset can test that.
  Stage 7 designs the experiment that would.
