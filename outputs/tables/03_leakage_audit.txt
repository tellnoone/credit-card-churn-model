==============================================================================
1. FEATURE-BY-FEATURE CLASSIFICATION
==============================================================================

  SAFE    : 18 features -> used by BOTH models
  SUSPECT : 14 features -> used by `all` only
  LEAKED  :  2 columns  -> dropped in dbt staging, never modelled

  The question asked of every feature: at the moment we would need to
  score a customer, does this value already exist, and is it free of
  information about the churn we are trying to predict?

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SAFE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * customer_age
      Age is a property of the person, not of their account behaviour. Known
      at application and updated by the calendar alone. Cannot be caused by
      churning. (Whether it should be USED is a fairness question, not a
      leakage one -- see plan section 8.)

  * gender
      Collected at application and immutable for this purpose, so it cannot be
      a consequence of churn. Same fairness caveat as age: SAFE here is a
      statement about leakage only, not about whether it should be used.

  * dependent_count
      Household composition, captured at application. Slow-moving and cannot
      plausibly be caused by a decision to close a credit card account.

  * education_level
      Captured at application and effectively fixed thereafter, so it cannot
      be caused by the churn it is used to predict. 15.0% 'Unknown', kept as
      its own level rather than imputed.

  * marital_status
      Captured at application and rarely refreshed afterwards, so it is stale
      rather than leaked -- it cannot be a consequence of churning. 7.4%
      'Unknown'.

  * income_category
      Stated income at application, banded rather than continuous, so it is
      coarse but stable and not refreshed by recent behaviour. 11.0%
      'Unknown'.

  * card_category
      Product held. Changes only on an explicit upgrade/downgrade event, which
      is a deliberate action rather than a symptom of disengagement.

  * card_category_ord
      Ordinal encoding of card_category on the issuer's own product ladder:
      Blue < Silver < Gold < Platinum. Inherits card_category's SAFE class.

  * months_on_book
      Tenure. Increases with the calendar. A churner's tenure stops growing at
      churn, which is a mild concern, but tenure is knowable at any point
      before churn so a model can use it prospectively.

  * tenure_years
      months_on_book expressed in years, which reads more naturally in a
      coefficient table. Same SAFE reasoning as months_on_book itself.

  * total_relationship_count
      Number of products held. A customer winding down MIGHT close products
      first, which would make this a symptom. Classified SAFE because product
      holdings are a deliberate cross-sell outcome and are the standard
      pre-period feature in retention models -- but this is the weakest SAFE
      call in the list, and is flagged as such rather than hidden.

  * credit_limit
      Set by the issuer's own risk policy at origination and on periodic
      review, not by the customer's recent activity, so it is an input to
      behaviour rather than an output of it.

  * products_per_tenure_year
      How fast the relationship deepened: products held per year of tenure.
      Derived from total_relationship_count and months_on_book, both SAFE.

  * education_level_ord
      Ordinal encoding of the education ladder; NULL where 'Unknown', with the
      signal carried by education_is_unknown instead.

  * income_category_ord
      Ordinal encoding of the stated-income bands; NULL where 'Unknown', with
      the signal carried by income_is_unknown instead.

  * education_is_unknown
      Missingness indicator for education. Carries the signal that the ordinal
      encoding drops when it emits NULL. Refusal to state a field is itself
      weakly informative (+0.93pp churn, measured).

  * income_is_unknown
      Missingness indicator for stated income (+0.84pp churn, measured). May
      also proxy an acquisition channel that did not collect it.

  * marital_is_unknown
      Missingness indicator for marital status (+1.25pp churn, measured -- the
      strongest of the three, though all are weak).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SUSPECT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * total_trans_ct
      Transactions over 'the last 12 months'. If that window runs up to the
      snapshot date, then for a customer who churned six months ago it
      includes the months they had already stopped transacting. The feature
      would then be measuring the churn, not anticipating it.

  * total_trans_amt
      Spend over the same undated 12-month window as total_trans_ct, and so
      carries exactly the same risk of straddling the churn event.

  * months_inactive_12_mon
      Months with no activity. For a churned customer, inactivity IS the
      churn. Also quirky: despite the name it maxes at 6 in this snapshot, an
      undocumented ceiling that is its own reason for caution.

  * contacts_count_12_mon
      Contacts with the bank. Plausibly a leading indicator (complaints before
      leaving) but equally plausibly the closure call itself. Without dates or
      contact reasons the two cannot be separated.

  * total_revolving_bal
      Revolving balance at snapshot. A closing customer pays down to zero, so
      a low balance may be a consequence of leaving rather than a cause.

  * avg_open_to_buy
      credit_limit minus revolving balance. Inherits the balance problem:
      arithmetically it is almost the complement of total_revolving_bal.

  * avg_utilization_ratio
      Balance over limit. Same reasoning as total_revolving_bal.

  * total_amt_chng_q4_q1
      Q4-vs-Q1 spend ratio. The sharpest case in the dataset: a collapse in
      recent spend versus earlier spend is close to a DEFINITION of
      disengagement rather than a predictor of it.

  * total_ct_chng_q4_q1
      Q4-vs-Q1 transaction-count ratio. Same argument as the amount ratio: a
      collapse in recent versus earlier activity may BE the churn signal
      rather than precede it.

  * avg_trans_amt
      Mean value per transaction. Derived from total_trans_amt and
      total_trans_ct, both SUSPECT, so it cannot be safer than its inputs.

  * revolving_to_limit
      Derived from total_revolving_bal (SUSPECT) and credit_limit (SAFE). A
      derived feature inherits the WORST class of its inputs.

  * spend_to_limit
      Spend as a share of the credit line. Derived from total_trans_amt, which
      is SUSPECT, so this inherits that class.

  * trans_per_active_month
      Transactions per month the customer was actually active. Derived from
      two SUSPECT columns (total_trans_ct and months_inactive_12_mon), so it
      compounds their window problem rather than escaping it.

  * q4_activity_collapsed
      Binary flag on total_ct_chng_q4_q1 < 0.5. Included precisely so the
      audit can show what a near-definition-of-churn feature does to the
      metrics.

==============================================================================
4b. HOW MUCH OF THE STRICT MODEL IS ONE FEATURE?
==============================================================================

  The strict model is weak. Before recommending it, it is worth asking
  where what little signal it has actually comes from.

  model                                        features    PR-AUC   ROC-AUC
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  random baseline                                     -    0.1607    0.5000
  strict (all SAFE features)                         18    0.2426    0.6201
  strict MINUS relationship-count features           16    0.2015    0.5709
  total_relationship_count ALONE                      1    0.2185    0.6124
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  total_relationship_count on its own reaches 0.2185 PR-AUC --
  90% of what the full 18-feature strict model achieves.
  Removing it (and its derivative) costs 0.0411 and leaves the model
  at ROC-AUC 0.5709, which is close to random.

  This is uncomfortable and worth saying plainly: the strict model is
  essentially ONE feature, and that feature is the one flagged in
  src/features.py as the weakest SAFE call in the list. A customer who
  is winding down their banking relationship may well close other
  products BEFORE closing the card -- in which case product count is a
  symptom too, and the strict model's small advantage over random is
  partly leakage of a subtler kind.

  The remaining 17 SAFE features -- every demographic, tenure, income
  and card-tier field -- add 0.0241 PR-AUC between them.
  Demographics barely predict churn here. That is a real finding, not a
  modelling failure, and it is consistent with what retention teams
  generally observe: behaviour predicts churn, attributes do not.

==============================================================================
5. RECOMMENDATION
==============================================================================

  Dropping the 14 SUSPECT features costs +0.7288 PR-AUC
  (+301.9% relative) on the LightGBM model.

  RECOMMENDED MODEL: strict.

  The reasoning, stated so it can be argued with:

  1. The gap does NOT tell us whether the suspect features are safe.
     A big gap is exactly what genuine predictive power looks like, and
     also exactly what leakage looks like. The two are empirically
     indistinguishable from performance alone.

  2. What settles it is how the data was generated, and there the
     snapshot is silent: no churn date, no observation window. We
     cannot demonstrate that a '12-month' aggregate ends before the
     churn it predicts. `months_inactive_12_mon` is the clearest case
     -- for a churned customer, inactivity IS the churn.

  3. The asymmetry of being wrong. A strict model that is too cautious
     under-performs by a known, measured amount. An `all` model built
     on leakage looks excellent in validation and fails silently in
     production, after the campaign budget has been committed.

  4. The strict model is deployable as specified. Every feature in it
     is available at scoring time for a customer who has not churned.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  BUT THE RECOMMENDATION COMES WITH A SERIOUS CAVEAT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The strict model is weak: PR-AUC 0.2426 against a 0.1607 base rate,
  ROC-AUC 0.6201. And per section 4b it is effectively one
  feature, which is itself of contestable safety.

  So the honest headline is NOT 'here is a churn model'. It is:

    This dataset cannot support a deployable churn model built only on
    features that are demonstrably known before churn. The 0.97 PR-AUC
    version is almost certainly reading the answer; the leak-free
    version barely beats guessing.

  That is a finding about the DATA, and it is the correct thing to
  report to a business. Shipping the 0.97 model would mean committing a
  retention budget against a number that will not survive contact with
  production.

  What a real deployment would do instead: keep the behavioural
  features, but compute them over a window that provably CLOSES before
  the prediction date -- transactions in months 1-6 predicting churn in
  months 7-12. That is standard practice and it works. It is impossible
  here only because this snapshot has no dates. The strict model is
  therefore a pessimistic lower bound, not an estimate of what a
  properly-built model would achieve.

  What would change this recommendation: a data dictionary showing the
  behavioural window closes before the churn observation window opens.
  That is a question for the data owner, not for the model.
